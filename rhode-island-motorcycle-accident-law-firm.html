National KIDS COUNT Data Book: Rhode Island ranks 26th in the 2026

Rhode Island ranks 26th among the 50 states for child well-being, according to the 2026 KIDS COUNT® Data Book released by the Annie E. Casey Foundation 1 , an annually published resource that tracks child well-being across the country. Every year, the Data Book presents national and state data from 16 indicators in four domains — economic well-being, education, health, and family and community factors — and ranks the states according to how children are faring overall and by domain. Rhode Island’s rank of 26th, based on 2024 data, is a drop from its rank of 20th based on 2023 data. From a press release For the first time this year, states receive a comprehensive score (from 0 to 1,000) in the Data Book, not just a ranking. The scores track 16 indicators in four domains — economic well-being, education, health, and family and community factors — over five years from 2019 to 2024. The new scoring system shows whether policies and public investment are actually improving children’s lives, not merely how states compare with one another. Rhode Island received a score of 583, slightly above the national score of 547. Rhode Island’s lower overall ranking is driven primarily by declines in Economic Well-Being and Family & Community Context. In fact, from 2019 to 2024, Rhode Island experienced the steepest decline in its Economic Well-Being score by a wide margin, plummeting by 161 points, while 29 states saw improvements in this area. The second-largest decline occurred in Nebraska, with a 93-point drop. At the other end of the spectrum, the greatest improvements were made by Delaware, New Mexico, and New Hampshire. Based on 2023 data, Rhode Island ranked 26th in Economic Well-Being and 14th in Family and Community. Using the most recent data from 2024, the state ranks 36th and 26th, respectively. Rhode Island saw stability in Education, with a score of 408, and improvement in Health score, moving from 682 to 737. In its 37th year of publication, the KIDS COUNT® Data Book provides reliable statewide data to help leaders see where progress is being made, where greater support is needed, and which strategies are making a difference. By offering both a big-picture view and a local road map, the Data Book equips policymakers, advocates, and communities with the information they need to make decisions that help kids and young people thrive throughout their lives. “Behind every number in this report is a child who is either hungry or fed, housed or homeless, progressing academically or falling behind. No state is consistently getting this right,” said Lisa M. Lawson , President and CEO of the Annie E. Casey Foundation. “The Data Book challenges us to follow the evidence and do what delivers results.” Rhode Island’s rankings for the four domains are below: Economic Well-Being: 36th Education: 25th Health: 11th Family & Community Context: 26th “We know what kids need to grow up healthy and connected so they can thrive as adults: Stable homes, strong schools, nutritious food, meaningful relationships, and opportunities to learn, play, and grow,” said Rhode Island KIDS COUNT 2 Executive Director Paige Parks . “Rhode Island needs to continue tackling the housing crisis. Housing in Rhode Island is considered among the least affordable in the country. We must also work to help families meet their economic needs through strategies like the proposed state Child Tax Credit and much-needed improvements to the Rhode Island Works program. We can see from this new methodology that the federal Child Tax Credit contributed to an increase in Rhode Island’s Economic Well-Being score between 2021 and 2022- the only increase in this domain over the five years.” Call to Action for Rhode Islanders Rhode Island KIDS COUNT encourages lawmakers and officials in Rhode Island to use this detailed information to unite and respond with initiatives that invest in children and families.
